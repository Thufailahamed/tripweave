# Keep SerpApi: cheaper hotel feeds cost more

Keep SerpApi as the production hotel-price source and do not promote the keyless Google Hotels scraper to primary status. At **1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 searches a month SerpApi costs $25, $150 and $725** on published plans, while the structured alternatives that look cheaper bill per hotel or per detail call and land at **$249–$5,000 depending on volume** for equivalent coverage; the raw-HTML scrapers that are genuinely cheaper shift parsing, proxy management and Google blocking onto us ([SerpApi pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing); [SearchApi pricing](https://www.searchapi.io/pricing); [Scrape.do pricing](https://scrape.do/pricing/)). Making the scraper fully production-grade is a **6–9 month, 0.5–1 FTE program** with a hard legal gate and no indemnity, and its current single-run coverage in the three-stop Sri Lanka sample is only **56–93%** ([internal notes](../docs/hotel-search-implementation.md)). The rational path is to cut SerpApi calls through caching, deduplication, qualification gating and retry hygiene, keep the scraper as disabled-by-default entity enrichment, and finish the Hotelbeds integration and add RateHawk if approved for bookable net rates. A **30-day, roughly $1,000 bake-off** can test SearchApi.io and Scrape.do before any contract changes; the evidence does not justify replacing SerpApi today.

## SerpApi's $725 month is the cheap end of the market

A "search" here means one destination plus check-in/check-out and occupancy query returning roughly 20 hotels. That definition matters because the vendors do not sell the same unit. SerpApi's Google Hotels listing returns `prices[].source` and `total_rate` for each property in one call, so one search prices dozens of options across Booking, Expedia, Agoda and others ([SerpApi Google Hotels](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-api)). SearchApi's listing does not include the OTA breakdown; Scrape.do's listing has no price at all; DataForSEO returns nightly prices, not totals. Those products look cheaper per call because the call does less work.

Read the table by its billing unit, not its headline. SearchApi's $40/10k listing plan is $0.004 per search, **73% below SerpApi's $150/10k**, but the listing has no OTA source names; adding the property endpoint turns a 20-hotel search into **21 billable calls**, so 10k searches cost $500, **3.3× SerpApi**, and 100k costs roughly $2,940–5,000, **4–7× SerpApi** ([SearchApi Hotels Property](https://www.searchapi.io/docs/google-hotels-property-api)). Scrape.do's $29/25k-call plan looks cheaper still, but its listing returns no prices; detail calls cost 10 credits each, so 10k searches cost $249 and 100k roughly $1,260–1,470, while OTA source names remain undocumented ([Scrape.do Google Hotels](https://scrape.do/products/ready-api/google-hotels-scraper/)). DataForSEO's $17.60/1k standard queue is the cheapest structured source of OTA names at 1k, but it returns nightly prices with no total, and the queue can take 45 minutes; live mode is $88/1k, 3.5× SerpApi ([DataForSEO pricing](https://dataforseo.com/pricing/business-data/google-hotels-api)). Bright Data is $30/1k, more than SerpApi at 1k and 3.6× at 100k. Makcorps is **14× SerpApi at 1k** because its $350 plan is the entry ticket, and Xotelo's per-hotel fan-out reaches roughly **$900 at 100k** before any SLA or commercial terms. The cheap numbers survive only if you ignore what the call returns.

SerpApi's own price ladder has cliffs that make percentage cuts misleading. A 30% reduction in calls saves **$0** at 1k, 10k or 100k; a 50% cut saves $0 at 1k, $75 at 10k and $0 at 100k; only an 80% cut produces material savings of $25, $75 and $450 respectively ([SerpApi pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing)). The worst boundary is 30,000 to 30,001 searches, where Big Data at $275 becomes Searcher at $725, a **164% jump**. The practical levers are therefore a local search cache, deduplication of identical destination/date/occupancy queries, gating searches to qualified enquiries, checkpointing successful stops so retries do not re-query them, and the startup discount of **50% for 12 months** if the account is eligible ([JoinSecret](https://joinsecret.com/serpapi)). SerpApi's built-in cache is free for exact query and parameter repeats within one hour, which rewards deterministic query construction ([SerpApi Google Hotels](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-api)).

Supply APIs invert the cost model but not the product problem. Hotelbeds, RateHawk, Expedia Rapid, Booking.com Demand, Agoda and Trip.com charge **no published per-search fee**; they monetise the booking through net-rate margin or commission, and they require a contract, certification or both ([Hotelbeds pricing models](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/pricing-models/); [RateHawk finance models](https://blog.ratehawk.com/types-of-ratehawk-finance-models/)). They are not interchangeable. Hotelbeds (subject to its `hotelMandatory` flag) and RateHawk offer net rates that a platform can mark up; Expedia is a sell-rate model with a `minimum_selling_price` floor; Booking.com prohibits caching prices or availability and prohibits using its content for price comparison or metasearch; Agoda's MSE/Lite model and Trip.com's affiliate API are redirect models that require a `landingUrl` or `BookURL`, while Agoda's full Demand API can complete a booking ([Hotelbeds pricing models](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/pricing-models/); [Expedia Shopping API](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/shopping/about-shopping-api); [Booking production checklist](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/development-guide/production-readiness); [Booking usage rules](https://legacy.developers.booking.com/api/commercial/index.html?page_url=permitted-use); [Agoda Search API](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs/json-search-api); [Agoda developer getting started](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs/getting-started); [Trip.com API docs](https://apidoc.trip.com/apidoc)).

The retail affiliate rates are roughly **4–7% at Agoda** and **5–7% at Trip.com**, while Booking.com splits **25–40% of its agency commission** with the partner ([Agoda affiliate agreement](https://partners.agoda.com/Content/pdf/Agoda_OnlineAffiliateAgreement.pdf); [Trip.com affiliate FAQ](https://www.trip.com/ask/questions/trip.com-affliate-program.html); [Booking partner agreement](https://image.email.partnerships.booking.com/lib/fe3011737364047d711079/m/1/55ae2c99-3af3-40b2-b57b-660c6baa6643.pdf)). Hotelbeds is the strongest asset the platform already has: the adapter exists, and the remaining work is an mTLS binding, certification and commercial terms. Hotelbeds covers 250,000 hotels and RateHawk quotes 3.3M accommodations in 220 countries, though neither publishes a Sri Lanka count ([Hotelbeds Hotels API](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/); [RateHawk API](https://www.ratehawk.com/lp/en-us/API/)). Workers can present a client certificate through an `mtls_certificates` binding, but mTLS fails against a destination that is itself Cloudflare-proxied, so verify the Hotelbeds endpoint before committing ([Cloudflare Workers mTLS](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/bindings/mtls/)). RateHawk is the most accessible net-rate alternative, with a free sandbox and roughly **6–10 weeks** to launch; its search rates have a recommended 30-minute lifetime and a 38-minute `match_hash`, so any booking flow must re-price before commit. Expedia is **12–20 weeks**, and Booking.com requires a managed affiliate contract ([RateHawk hotelpage docs](https://docs.emergingtravel.com/docs/affiliate-api/hotel-search/retrieve-hotelpage/); [Hotelbeds mTLS](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/mutual-authentication/); [RateHawk sandbox](https://blog.ratehawk.com/introducing-the-ratehawk-api-sandbox/); [Expedia getting started](https://developers.expediagroup.com/docs/products/rapid/setup/getting-started); [Booking prerequisites](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/prerequisites)). Amadeus Self-Service was decommissioned on **17 July 2026**; only Enterprise remains, so the existing adapter is dead for new credentials and should be removed from the near-term plan ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/amadeus-shut-down-self-service-apis-portal-developers)). These APIs can replace SerpApi's **bookability**, not its **cross-OTA comparison**. At zero bookings their cash cost is near zero, but so is their value; at 100k searches the cost depends on conversion and booking value, not search count.

## Only one feed returns OTA names and totals in a single call

The field table explains why the headline prices above are not comparable. SerpApi is the only reviewed provider whose **listing** response gives OTA source names and a total-stay price together, plus `property_token`, ratings, amenities and cancellation at search level; its separate property-details endpoint adds room-level prices ([SerpApi Google Hotels](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-api); [SerpApi Property Details](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-property-details)). No Google Hotels SERP provider exposes a normalised board-basis enum. Breakfast sometimes appears as a property amenity or a free-text feature, which is not the same as a rate-level meal plan. That distinction is not cosmetic: in the platform's own 25-property sample, only **10/25 (40%)** exposed an explicit breakfast-included rate, **9/25 (36%)** exposed only room-only rates and **6/25 (24%)** exposed only compact or unknown rates ([internal notes](../docs/hotel-search-implementation.md)). A property-level "Free breakfast" label was not proof of a breakfast-included rate for Heaven Seven Hotel Kandy or Villa Surf Beach. Any Google Hotels SERP provider that does not return rate-level board evidence cannot satisfy a strict breakfast quote, however cheap its per-search price. Even SerpApi's feed has no room-count parameter; the current provider prices the largest requested room and scales the total by the number of rooms, so multi-room quotes remain an approximation until a supply API revalidates them ([internal notes](../docs/hotel-search-implementation.md)). The scraper is not field-poor, either: its entity parser exposes room name, occupancy, cancellation and board basis where Google publishes them, and its search cards carry source and price. The problem is incomplete coverage and unhedged legal/operational risk, not a missing schema.

Coverage is the second trap. The custom scraper's nine live runs across Kandy, Ella and Galle produced single-run priced coverage of **88%, 56% and 93%** against SerpApi; merging three runs improved card counts from 19→33, 19→34 and 20→34 but still did not cover every SerpApi property ([internal notes](../docs/hotel-search-implementation.md)). Entity pages were reliable in the same sample — **22/22 fetches succeeded, median 787 ms, p95 1,045 ms** — but they missed cheaper OTA rates that SerpApi found: Hotel Suisse Kandy showed Booking.com at **668** versus SerpApi's **324** from Super.com, and Le Grand Galle showed Expedia at **1,121** versus SerpApi's **898** from Reservation Day. Xotelo's live Sri Lanka check proves the underlying OTAs price the market — **1,936 properties in Kandy, 804 in Colombo**, with Booking 204, Agoda 138, Trip.com 93 and Vio 137 USD for the same Kandy hotel and dates — but the **2.2× spread** and `tax: null` on all four offers show it is not a normalised room/board comparison ([Xotelo live rate query](https://data.xotelo.com/api/rates?hotel_key=g304138-d25367549&chk_in=2026-11-10&chk_out=2026-11-12&rooms=1&adults=2&currency=USD)). Apify's `jy-labs` actor is disqualified outright for this market: its 22-country list excludes Sri Lanka and the Maldives ([Apify jy-labs](https://apify.com/jy-labs/google-hotels-multi-query-scraper)). No vendor publishes Sri Lanka-specific API coverage, so relative depth in Colombo, Galle, Kandy, Ella, Negombo or the Maldives cannot be verified without a paid pilot; absence of a country count is not evidence of poor coverage.

Production readiness is the third gap. SerpApi is a synchronous REST API with plan throughput from **200 to 20,000 searches per hour on the tiers modelled here**, a one-hour cache and an existing production integration ([SerpApi pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing); [SerpApi Google Hotels](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-api); [internal notes](../docs/hotel-search-implementation.md)); SearchApi advertises a 99.9% SLA but caps usage at **20% of monthly credits per hour**; DataForSEO advertises 99.95% uptime and 10,000 calls per minute but limits live mode to 30 simultaneous requests; Scrape.do advertises 99.98% success with 10–200 concurrent requests; Bright Data offers unlimited concurrency but runs datasets asynchronously and requires KYC before residential or mobile network use; Apify actors are asynchronous and need webhooks or polling; Oxylabs and ScrapingBee are synchronous raw-HTML fetchers with concurrency caps (25–200 for ScrapingBee), so they are production-ready as fetchers, not as hotel-data products ([SearchApi pricing](https://www.searchapi.io/pricing); [DataForSEO rate limits](https://dataforseo.com/help-center/rate-limits-and-request-limits); [Scrape.do pricing](https://scrape.do/pricing/); [Bright Data residential pricing](https://brightdata.com/pricing/proxy-network/residential-proxies); [Apify pricing](https://apify.com/pricing); [ScrapingBee pricing](https://www.scrapingbee.com/pricing/); [Oxylabs Google Travel Hotels](https://developers.oxylabs.io/api-targets/search-engines/google/travel-hotels)). Cloudflare Workers can call all of them with native `fetch`, but a 21-call search fits awkwardly against the **6 simultaneous outgoing connections** limit and should run in Queues or Workflows at volume ([Cloudflare Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Makcorps publishes no API-specific SLA, rate limit or caching guidance, and its public terms are generic 2021 website terms; Xotelo is an individual-run service with no terms page or SLA found ([Makcorps terms](https://www.makcorps.com/terms/); [Xotelo](https://xotelo.com/)).

The legal layer is the one that cannot be engineered away. Google's robots.txt, fetched on **27 October 2026**, disallows `/travel/search` — the exact discovery endpoint the scraper uses — along with `/travel/entity`, `/travel/lodging/clk`, `/hotelfinder/rpc` and `/hotels/rpc` ([Google robots.txt](https://www.google.com/robots.txt)). One nuance matters for accuracy: Google Hotels property pages live under `/travel/hotels/entity/...`, which the `/travel/entity` prefix does **not** match, so the entity-enrichment path is less clearly prohibited by robots.txt than the search path; the distinction is often overstated. The nuance does not rescue the design. Google's Terms of Service, effective **30 July 2026**, separately prohibit "using automated means to access content from any of our services in violation of the machine-readable instructions on our web pages", "bypassing our systems or protective measures" and "hiding or misrepresenting who you are in order to violate these terms" ([Google Terms of Service](https://policies.google.com/terms)). The search path is squarely disallowed, and the ToS violation stands on its own.

Google is also litigating a strikingly similar playbook. It sued SerpApi in the Northern District of California on **19 December 2025** (Case No. 4:25-cv-10826), alleging copyright infringement and DMCA §1201 circumvention of its SearchGuard anti-scraping system, which Google deployed in January 2025 ([Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/safety-security/serpapi-lawsuit/); [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/848365-google-scraper-lawsuit-serpapi); [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72059948/google-llc-v-serpapi-llc/)). The first motion to dismiss was granted with leave to amend on **20 July 2026**; Google filed an amended complaint, SerpApi filed a second motion to dismiss on **24 August 2026**, the hearing was vacated on **5 October 2026**, and the motion was pending as of the docket's last update on **24 October 2026**. The case is at the pleading stage, so neither the merits nor Google's ultimate success is established. The business risk is nonetheless real: a small agency is more likely to receive a cease-and-desist, an IP or account block, or a proxy-vendor termination than a lawsuit, and any of those is business-ending if the scraper is the primary price source. Google's own hotel APIs do not offer a read-side shortcut: they are supply-side tools for sending prices and content to Google or participating in Hotel Ads, not a public API for consuming aggregated OTA prices ([Google Hotel APIs](https://developers.google.com/hotels)).

The case law is mixed, which is why the risk is contractual rather than purely criminal. *Van Buren* narrowed the CFAA, *hiQ* made public-data scraping hard to treat as unauthorised access, and *Ryanair v. Booking.com* produced a $5,000 CFAA verdict that the court overturned because only **$2,457.72** of qualifying loss was proved ([Van Buren](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-783_k53l.pdf); [hiQ](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/04/18/17-16783.pdf); [Ryanair analysis](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2025/03/court-overturns-a-bad-jury-verdict-against-scraping-ryanair-v-booking-guest-blog-post.htm)). *Meta v. Bright Data* shows a breach-of-contract claim can fail when the terms, as written, do not cover logged-off scraping of public data ([Quinn Emanuel analysis](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-what-does-the-meta-v-bright-data-summary-judgment-ruling-mean-for-web-scraping/)). Google's terms are broader and its robots.txt is explicit, so breach-of-contract and §1201 claims remain live even where CFAA and copyright claims are weak.

In the EU, the commercial text-and-data-mining exception requires lawful access and allows a robots.txt opt-out; in the UK, the copyright exception is limited to non-commercial research ([EU DSM Directive Art. 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790); [UK CDPA s.29A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/29A)). Displaying OTA prices adds a consumer-law layer: EU and UK rules require the total price upfront, including VAT and mandatory fees, and a price you cannot honour can be a misleading commercial practice ([EU Price Indication Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/price-indications-on-consumer-products.html); [UK DMCCA guidance](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/04/dmcca-drip-pricing)). Google's own Hotel Center Price Accuracy Policy sets that standard for its hotel partners: prices must include taxes and fees and be updated several times a day, and a long-lived scrape cache would not meet it even if the access were licensed ([Google Price Accuracy Policy](https://support.google.com/hotelprices/answer/6064419?hl=en)).

A cheaper scraping vendor does not transfer that risk. SearchApi's terms prohibit reproducing, duplicating, copying, selling or reselling the service, and its legal guarantee expressly excludes the customer's "use, storage, redistribution, or commercialization of data"; SerpApi's terms similarly prohibit resale and its $2M Legal Shield covers lawful scraping and parsing, not how the data is used, and is available only above the Developer plan ([SearchApi terms](https://www.searchapi.io/legal/terms); [SerpApi legal](https://serpapi.com/legal)). Bright Data's master agreement prohibits reselling the service or creating derivative works without written authorisation; Apify's actor terms give the customer rights in the output but do not cure the underlying Google rights; Scrape.do's terms grant no display licence; Makcorps' 2021 terms restrict commercial use and public display of "the materials" ([Bright Data MSA](https://brightdata.com/license); [Apify Actor Terms](https://docs.apify.com/legal/actor-terms-and-conditions); [Scrape.do Terms](https://scrape.do/terms-of-use/); [Makcorps terms](https://www.makcorps.com/terms/)). DataForSEO's terms are silent on caching and display, Oxylabs' terms could not be retrieved, and ScrapingBee's terms do not grant a display right; silence is not permission ([DataForSEO terms](https://dataforseo.com/terms-of-service); [ScrapingBee terms](https://www.scrapingbee.com/terms-and-conditions/)). None of these providers can grant rights Google has not granted. The cleanest route to a public price comparison remains a direct OTA affiliate or partner agreement with explicit display and attribution terms, or a licensed aggregator that can prove those rights — a commercial negotiation, not a scraper upgrade.

## Cheap per-request prices hide detail calls, parsers and legal risk

The detail-call multiplier is the first reason the headline prices mislead. A 20-hotel search is one billable unit at SerpApi but **21** at SearchApi and Scrape.do once every hotel needs a property or detail call, and **22** at DataForSEO once two SERPs of up to 18 hotels are combined with 20 hotel-info lookups. The multiplier is not a modelling quirk; it follows from the documented schemas. SearchApi's listing lacks `prices[].source`, Scrape.do's listing lacks prices entirely, and DataForSEO's `hotel_searches` returns per-night prices with a null `items` array. A team that budgets from the listing price will discover the gap only after integration, when the per-hotel fan-out is already in the code.

| Volume | SerpApi | Self-host proxies (3 runs) | Self-host + 0.5 FTE | Generic scraper (ScraperAPI, 3 runs) | One-off legal review |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1k/mo | $25 | $5.58–11.16 (min $30) | ~$6,030 | $49 | $15k–50k |
| 10k/mo | $150 | $55.80–111.60 | ~$6,070 | $149 | — |
| 100k/mo | $725 | $558–1,116 | ~$6,580–7,220 | $975 | — |
| 1M/mo | $3,750 | $3,488–6,975 | ~$9,490–12,980 | ~$9,750 | — |

The TCO model assumes 310 KB per search page on the wire, 1.5× request overhead for retries, three runs to approach SerpApi coverage, residential bandwidth at $4–8/GB, and 0.5 FTE loaded at **$6,000/month**; it excludes bad-price incidents, refunds, chargebacks and revenue loss from outages ([productionization assessment](../research_notes/Cheap%20hotel%20price%20data%20providers/scraper-productionization.md)). At 100k searches, the 0.5 FTE alone is **8× the SerpApi bill**. At 1M, SerpApi is $3,750 while self-hosting plus the same 0.5 FTE is $9,490–12,980, still **2.5–3.5×** more. The only way to make self-hosting cheaper is to exclude engineering, use the cheapest committed bandwidth and assume no legal event — three assumptions that do not survive a production review.

Proxy cost is real but it is the smallest part of the build. A Google Hotels search page measured **2,787,014 decoded bytes and 310,292 brotli bytes**; an entity page measured **2,368,941 decoded bytes**, roughly 260 KB compressed at the same ratio ([internal assessment](../research_notes/Cheap%20hotel%20price%20data%20providers/scraper-productionization.md)). At 100k searches, one run costs **$186–372** in residential bandwidth, but single-run coverage is only 56–93%; three runs cost **$558–1,116**, already 77–154% of the entire SerpApi bill before a parser, a queue, a canary or an on-call rotation. Bright Data's smallest residential commitment is **$499/month for 141 GB**, Oxylabs' entry plan is **$30/month** and it explicitly restricts "some Google domains" on its residential network, and Bright Data requires KYC before residential or mobile use ([Bright Data residential](https://brightdata.com/pricing/proxy-network/residential-proxies); [Oxylabs residential](https://oxylabs.io/pricing/residential-proxy-pool)). A managed unblocker such as Bright Data Web Unlocker is cheaper per request — **$1.50/1,000**, or about **$450** for 100k three-run — but it is still Google scraping with no legal shield, and the vendor may be blocked or enjoined ([Bright Data Web Unlocker](https://brightdata.com/pricing/web-unlocker)). The bandwidth estimate assumes vendors bill compressed bytes; if a vendor bills decompressed bytes, proxy cost rises roughly ninefold and the case against self-hosting gets stronger, not weaker.

Engineering is the dominant cost and the one most often omitted from vendor comparisons. A credible production system needs a versioned parser with golden fixtures, raw-HTML archiving, a canary every 5–15 minutes from multiple egress locations, schema-drift alerts, per-IP circuit breakers, a multi-layer cache, a fallback provider, observability and an incident runbook. That is **0.5–1 FTE of ongoing work**, plus on-call for price incidents, plus **1–3 days/month** of parser maintenance and **2–6 emergency parser breaks a year** by the planning assumption ([productionization assessment](../research_notes/Cheap%20hotel%20price%20data%20providers/scraper-productionization.md)). Cloudflare supplies the primitives — Workers, Queues, Workflows, Durable Objects, Cache API, R2, Tail Workers, Browser Rendering — but not a static Worker egress IP, so the IP-reputation problem remains ([Cloudflare Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/); [Cloudflare dedicated egress IPs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/traffic-controls/egress-policies/dedicated-egress-ips/)). Browser Rendering is cheap at **$0.09/browser-hour**, but a headless browser from Cloudflare's own ranges does not solve a block that is primarily IP- and fingerprint-based ([Cloudflare Browser Rendering pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-run/pricing/)).

The legal cost is asymmetric and does not appear in any per-request table. A single cease-and-desist, injunction or proxy-vendor termination can cost more in legal fees and lost bookings than years of SerpApi. A one-off legal review is estimated at **$15k–50k**; that is **1.7–5.7 years** of SerpApi at 100k searches, before any defence costs. SerpApi's $2M Legal Shield is not insurance for intentional circumvention, but it is a contractual allocation of risk that self-hosting lacks, and it is the reason the premium is better understood as a risk-transfer fee than a data fee ([SerpApi legal](https://serpapi.com/legal)).

The honest case for building is control, not cost. If the team has idle senior engineering capacity, a strategic requirement to own the price pipeline, a legal memo that accepts the residual risk, and a credible plan to reach **98% coverage** of SerpApi's priced properties, then a 6–9 month build could be rational as an insurance policy against vendor disruption. That is a different decision from "SerpApi is expensive." It should be funded as a strategic program with a named owner, a legal gate at week 2 and week 24, and a kill switch — not as a side project behind a feature flag. And the cheaper form of redundancy is a second contracted provider, not a prohibited scraper.

## A 30-day bake-off can settle the vendor question

The bake-off should be narrow enough to finish and broad enough to catch the failure modes that vendor documentation hides. Use **30 properties**: 10 in Colombo, 5 on the Galle/south coast, 5 in Kandy or Ella, 5 in Negombo and 5 in the Maldives. Price each for **7 nights, 2 adults**, with `gl=lk`, `hl=en`, and both **LKR and USD**, across four date windows — roughly 30, 60, 90 and 180 days out — to capture seasonality and last-minute behaviour. That yields **240 query units per provider**. For the fan-out providers, budget about **5,000 calls** per full pass; run each query three times to measure stability, not just a single lucky response.

| Workstream | Days | What we do | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corpus freeze | 1–3 | Lock the 30 properties, four date windows, two currencies and exact query strings | Reproducible test set |
| Baseline and truth | 3–5 | Run SerpApi; manually capture 10 OTA booking-page totals, taxes, board and occupancy | Ground-truth sample |
| Challenger runs | 6–12 | SearchApi full, Scrape.do full, DataForSEO live + standard, ScrapingBee, Oxylabs, Bright Data, Apify `solidcode`, Xotelo, Makcorps trial, self-host three-run shadow | Raw datasets |
| Stability | 13–18 | Three repeats per query; record p50/p95 latency, error/block rate, empty results | Reliability profile |
| Audit | 19–24 | Compare 10 offers per provider against the OTA page; price, tax, board, occupancy, cancellation | Accuracy scorecard |
| Decision | 25–30 | TCO at 1k/10k/100k, legal terms, operational fit | Go/no-go memo |

The API spend for this design is roughly **$850–1,000** at list prices for three repeats: SerpApi Production at $150 for the Legal Shield, SearchApi Production at $100, Scrape.do Hobby at $29, DataForSEO live and standard at about $76, Bright Data at about $14, Oxylabs Starter at $30, ScrapingBee Hobby at $19, Apify `solidcode` at about $14, Xotelo Ultra at about $100 and Makcorps at $350 if the 30-call trial is insufficient. Engineering effort is **6–10 engineer-days**; legal review of the winner's terms runs in parallel and is not optional. Apify `jy-labs` is excluded from the Sri Lanka test because its country list does not include Sri Lanka; it can be run as a schema control in India or Singapore, but that does not validate the market we need.

| Gate | Pass threshold | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | ≥95% to advance; ≥98% before traffic | A cheaper feed with 56–93% coverage is not a replacement |
| OTA breadth | ≥2 named OTAs for 90% of priced properties | Cross-OTA comparison is the product |
| Total-stay price | ≥95% of priced properties | Nightly-only prices cannot be quoted |
| Board-basis evidence | ≥90% rate-level presence; ≥99% precision on audit | Amenities are not rates |
| Price parity | Median ≤2%, p95 ≤5% vs OTA page | Bad quotes cost more than API fees |
| Latency | p95 ≤3 seconds for live calls | User-facing search |
| Reliability | Error/block rate ≤2% over three repeats | Production readiness |
| Cost | ≤50% of SerpApi at target volume | The only reason to switch |
| Legal | Written display and caching licence | Hard gate; no licence, no switch |
| Operations | SLA, status page, support, canary capability | Hard gate |

The decision rules should be written before the test, not after. If no provider passes the legal, coverage and parity gates, keep SerpApi and stop; the exercise has still produced a useful benchmark. If one provider passes but the saving is under **$200/month** at the current volume, keep SerpApi and revisit when volume passes roughly **30,000 calls/month** or SerpApi changes its price or terms. If one provider passes and the saving is over **$500/month**, or a strategic need justifies the migration, run a **90-day parallel** at 5–10% of traffic, then shift 25%, 50% and 100% only if parity holds. Self-hosting should advance only with a written legal memo and **98% coverage**; otherwise it remains an internal enrichment tool. Even if SerpApi remains primary, run the SearchApi leg as continuity insurance: the Google lawsuit is a reminder that one vendor can become a single point of failure.

What would change this recommendation is specific: SerpApi raising prices by more than 3×, a supply disruption lasting more than 30 days, an adverse legal ruling that makes SerpApi unusable, volume above **250,000 searches/month** where a validated challenger or negotiated contract could save more than **$1,000/month**, or a challenger offering a written display licence plus 98% price parity. None of those conditions is true today. The bake-off is the cheap way to know when one becomes true.

## Production-grade scraping is a six-to-nine-month program

The current scraper is a standalone Node spike with unit tests, not a production system. The standalone script has no proxy layer, no persistent raw-HTML archive, no canary, no drift alerts, no queue and no production observability; the integrated enrichment path adds a 15-second timeout, exponential backoff, consent-wall detection, a three-failure circuit breaker and a 15-minute cache, but not the rest ([internal notes](../docs/hotel-search-implementation.md); [productionization assessment](../research_notes/Cheap%20hotel%20price%20data%20providers/scraper-productionization.md)). Its search coverage is 56–93%, and its entity pages miss cheaper OTA sources. The gap is not a parser bug. It is the operating system around the parser: the ability to detect a break, prove what changed, replay the old HTML, fail over to a contracted source and stop showing bad prices.

A credible build has six workstreams, and each one has a gate before the next begins.

| Workstream | Minimum deliverable | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Legal | Written memo on robots.txt, ToS, DMCA §1201, EU/UK access and OTA display terms | Go/no-go in week 2 |
| Fetch | Proxy abstraction, per-IP concurrency 1–2, jittered backoff, `Retry-After`, circuit breaker, Queues/Workflows | 99.5% success at target volume |
| Parser | Semver plus `fixtureHash`, R2 raw-HTML archive, 30–50 golden fixtures across 8–10 destinations and 2–3 locales, contract tests | Parse success ≥99%; drift alerts |
| Data quality | Occupancy ≥ party, rate-level board evidence, all-in total, quote TTL, cross-source reconciliation | 100% of quotes have evidence; breakfast precision ≥99% |
| Coverage | Three-run union, shadow comparison against SerpApi on a stratified sample | ≥98% of SerpApi priced properties |
| Operations | Canary every 5–15 minutes from three ASNs, dashboards, runbook, one-flag kill switch, on-call | SLOs met for four weeks |

The phased path runs from a legal gate to conditional production in about 24 weeks, with a further buffer before primary traffic. Phase 0, weeks 1–2, quantifies actual volume and obtains the legal memo; if the memo says the robots.txt/ToS/§1201 risk is unacceptable, stop, because engineering cannot fix that. Phase 1, weeks 3–6, builds the fetch layer. Phase 2, weeks 7–10, productises the parser and runs a 1% shadow. Phase 3, weeks 11–14, hardens data quality and audits 100–200 quotes against OTA booking pages. Phase 4, weeks 15–18, load-tests at 10k and 100k, runs failover drills and installs cost controls. Phase 5, weeks 19–24, shifts 25% then 50% of traffic with a four-week parallel run, legal sign-off and an incident runbook. The realistic estimate to shift **primary** traffic is **6–9 months**, not 24 weeks, because legal review, hiring and the first two parser breaks consume the buffer.

The acceptance gates are strict for a reason. A search success rate of **≥99.5% over seven days**, **≥99.9%** of displayed quotes passing validation, p95 search latency **<2.5 seconds**, **<0.5%** stale quotes at checkout, 100% of quotes with explicit occupancy, board-basis evidence, tax-inclusive total and timestamp, and coverage **≥98%** of SerpApi's priced properties with a median price delta **≤2%** and p95 **≤5%** ([productionization assessment](../research_notes/Cheap%20hotel%20price%20data%20providers/scraper-productionization.md)). A parser that returns HTTP 200 with wrong prices is worse than an outage, because it silently corrupts quotes. That is why the build needs price-integrity SLOs and on-call, not just uptime monitoring.

The cost is what makes this a strategic decision rather than a procurement one. At 100k searches, three-run residential proxies cost **$558–1,116**, Cloudflare and observability add roughly **$20–100**, and 0.5 FTE adds **$6,000/month**, for **~$6,580–7,220/month** before the one-off **$15k–50k** legal review and before any incident. SerpApi at the same volume is **$725/month**. At 1M searches, SerpApi is **$3,750/month** and self-hosting plus 0.5 FTE is **$9,490–12,980/month**. There is no modelled volume up to 1M searches where self-hosting wins on a fully loaded basis; the apparent saving at 100k exists only if the engineer is free and the legal risk is ignored.

That is the direct answer to "can we make it fully production-grade because SerpApi is expensive": yes, it can be built, but it is a **6–9 month, 0.5–1 FTE program with legal gates**, and it is **not recommended as the primary source at current volumes**. The current integration makes one SerpApi call per stop for three-stop itineraries. The repository does not record the monthly invoice, but at a few hundred to a few thousand itineraries a month the bill is in the tens to low hundreds of dollars, and the absolute saving from replacing it cannot repay a migration, let alone a build. The scraper's defensible role is the one already implemented: disabled-by-default entity enrichment for properties SerpApi has already returned, used to verify board basis and room details, with no scraped-only prices shown to consumers. In the strict-breakfast pilot, **15/15** enriched fetches succeeded, **six** exposed explicit breakfast rates, and all five packages were feasible within the $4,000 budget — that is the scraper earning its keep as a verifier, not as a price source ([internal notes](../docs/hotel-search-implementation.md)). The pilot's own legal sign-off and two-week live measurement are still open; keep the flag off until those gates close.

## Conclusion

The expensive part of hotel pricing is not the API call. It is owning an undocumented dependency: a parser that can break without notice, a proxy pool that can be poisoned or terminated, a legal position that no vendor contract can cure, and an on-call rotation that has to care when a price is wrong. SerpApi's premium buys a maintained parser, a one-hour cache, throughput guarantees, structured OTA and total-stay fields and a $2M legal shield; at 100k searches it costs less than four engineer-days a month. That is why the "cheaper" search keeps returning the same answer: SearchApi and Scrape.do move the cost into detail calls, DataForSEO moves it into latency and missing totals, ScrapingBee and Oxylabs move it into parsing and proxies, Makcorps and Xotelo move it into missing fields, Bright Data and Apify move it into per-record billing and unverified schemas, and none of them moves the legal risk.

The durable architecture is two layers, not one vendor. Keep a contracted comparison layer for discovery — SerpApi today, with a qualified second source as continuity insurance and a switch only if the bake-off proves parity and the volume justifies it — and build a bookable supply layer with Hotelbeds first and RateHawk if approved. Confine the scraper to internal entity enrichment, where it already earns its keep by verifying board basis and room details, and revisit the build only when volume, price or legal triggers change the arithmetic. The 30-day bake-off is the cheapest way to keep that option alive without mistaking activity for progress.

## Addendum: ScraperCompany (scrapercompany.com)

Checked 27 October 2026. ScraperCompany is a new, independent scraping API (not ScraperAPI) with a Google Hotels endpoint, a 330-night price calendar, OTA endpoints and a broad set of other scrapers. It is cheaper than SerpApi on the headline Google Hotels search call, but not for our full requirement.

| Flow | Credits per stop | 1k stops/mo | 10k stops/mo | 100k stops/mo |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ScraperCompany search only | 4 | $49 (Starter minimum) | $49 (Starter) | $399 (Scale) |
| Search + property pages for top 5 | 29 | $49 (Starter) | $399 (Scale) | ~$1,998 (two Business plans) |
| Search + property pages for 20 | 104 | $149 (Growth) | $999 (Business) | Enterprise |
| SerpApi, like-for-like | 1 | $25 | $150 | $725 |

Sources and prices: [Google Hotels API](https://scrapercompany.com/google-hotels-api), [pricing](https://scrapercompany.com/pricing), [OpenAPI](https://scrapercompany.com/openapi.json), checked 27 Oct 2026.

**What is genuinely attractive**

- The search call is 4 credits: **$0.0049 on Starter, $0.0040 on Growth, $0.0032 on Scale, $0.0027 on Business**. That undercuts SerpApi by about 3× at 10k searches and 45% at 100k, if search alone is enough.
- Failed calls and empty pages are free; credits are valid for 12 months; unused packs can be refunded within 60 days; the 50-call trial needs no card.
- The API is SERP-compatible, documented with an OpenAPI spec, and separates base rate, taxes, fees and total with provenance. It returns `property_token`, so our existing entity enrichment can still use the same token.
- The 330-night calendar is useful for rate monitoring and is far cheaper than 330 SerpApi searches.

**Why it is not a drop-in replacement for us**

- The search response gives a headline price and amenities, but `sources[]` seller rows are "mostly vacation rentals, usually empty for hotels." It does not reliably give the per-OTA source that SerpApi's `prices[]` gives.
- The property response returns `all_offers` with seller, room name, price and cancellation, but the schema has **no meal-plan or board-basis field**. Room descriptions are free text. It therefore cannot replace our entity enrichment for strict breakfast; at best it feeds it.
- Once you add five property pages per stop to recover seller/room detail, the cost is 29 credits per stop: **$399 at 10k stops vs SerpApi's $150**, and about **$1,998 at 100k vs $725**. The cheap headline only survives if you give up the detail we need.
- It is a scraper, not a licensed data feed. Its terms put compliance with the source sites' terms and the right to use the data on the customer; there is no legal shield, no SLA and no SOC 2. The API is based in Canada, but the terms contemplate a future transfer to an incorporated company.
- The domain was registered on **26 September 2026**; the site is about one month old. Its Apify profile has 92 total users, 42 monthly users, no ratings and an 11-day response time. Its status page shows a 90-day uptime graph despite the domain being younger than that, so treat the uptime claim as unproven. No independent reviews or track record were found.

**Verdict**

Cheap for **search-only** at roughly 10k+ stops/month; not cheaper for the full SerpApi-plus-board-basis flow, and not yet good enough to be the primary production dependency. The right next step is a 50-call trial on 10–20 Sri Lanka properties: compare property overlap, total-price parity, OTA-source presence, board-basis evidence and p95 latency against SerpApi. If it passes, use it as a shadow/secondary search source or as a search-only primary with our entity enrichment retained; keep SerpApi for quote-grade validation until the vendor has a track record, an SLA and a legal position we can rely on.
