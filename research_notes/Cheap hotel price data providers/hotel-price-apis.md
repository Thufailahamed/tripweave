# Hotel price and availability APIs vs SerpApi Google Hotels

Research note for a small TypeScript/Cloudflare Workers travel platform that already integrates Hotelbeds and Amadeus. Current vendor list prices were read from the vendor's public page on **2026-10-27** unless a different date is stated; prices for closed or historical products are marked as such. "Cross-OTA" means one response contains prices for the same property from multiple online travel agencies; "single-supplier" means the API only sells its own or its suppliers' inventory.

Platform context (internal, not a web source): the repo's `docs/hotel-search-implementation.md` says SerpApi Google Hotels is the production discovery/pricing provider for Sri Lanka stops (Kandy, Ella, Galle) and recommends keeping it; the keyless Google Hotels spike is documented as a legal/ToS risk. `docs/architecture.md` says Hotelbeds is implemented against APItude shapes but still needs credentials/certification; Hotelbeds' own docs additionally require mTLS for availability and booking. The Amadeus Self-Service adapter was written against the published specs.

## 1. What each provider returns, and how they compare on coverage, access, price, and effort

### Takeaway
Only three providers return cross-OTA prices without a supply contract: **SerpApi** (Google Hotels), **Makcorps** (aggregated 200+ OTA prices), and **Xotelo** (TripAdvisor-derived OTA rates). Of those, only SerpApi is a production-grade, licensed, self-serve feed. Everything else — RateHawk, Expedia Rapid, Booking.com Demand, Agoda, Trip.com, Hotelbeds, Amadeus — is single-supplier inventory behind a partnership, and Amadeus Self-Service is now closed.

### Provider detail

**SerpApi Google Hotels.** SerpApi's Google Hotels engine returns, per property, `rate_per_night`, `total_rate`, a `prices` array with source and cancellation terms, plus `overall_rating`, `reviews`, `amenities`, `gps_coordinates` and `images` ([SerpApi Google Hotels API](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-api); [independent 2026 comparison](https://flightpowers.com/guides/best-hotel-data-apis-2026)). A separate Property Details endpoint takes a `property_token` and returns property information plus room-level prices ([SerpApi Google Hotels Property Details](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-property-details)). It is self-serve: an API key, no partner application. Pricing on 2026-10-27 was $25 for 1,000 searches, $75 for 5,000, $150 for 15,000, $275 for 30,000, $725 for 100,000 and $2,750 for 500,000; only successful searches count, and cached/errored/failed searches are free ([SerpApi pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing)). The built-in cache expires after one hour ([SerpApi Google Hotels API](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-api)). Production plans and above carry a U.S. Legal Shield of up to $2 million for the scraping/parsing, but not for how the data is used, and search data is retained for 31 days unless ZeroTrace is enabled (the API docs label it "Enterprise only"; the pricing page shows it on Cloud plans of 1M+ searches/month) ([SerpApi legal](https://serpapi.com/legal); [SerpApi Google Hotels API](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-api); [SerpApi pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing)). Sri Lanka is covered: the platform already uses it for Kandy/Ella/Galle pricing, and its own implementation note recommends keeping SerpApi as the production discovery provider because the keyless Google Hotels spike has ToS/legal risk (internal `docs/hotel-search-implementation.md`).

**Makcorps.** Makcorps advertises prices from 200+ OTAs. The hotel-ID endpoint is documented to return "more than 15 cheapest vendors per hotel"; the city endpoint returns all hotels in a city with the top four cheapest vendors; it uses its own hotel IDs, so an integration needs its mapping API ([Makcorps Hotel Price APIs](https://www.makcorps.com/documentation/hotel-price-apis/); [hotel-ID endpoint](https://www.makcorps.com/documentation/hotel-api-search-by-hotel-id/)). The documented response is only `vendor`, `price` and `tax` — there is no room type, board basis, cancellation policy, availability count, rate key, timestamp or booking URL ([Makcorps hotel-ID endpoint](https://www.makcorps.com/documentation/hotel-api-search-by-hotel-id/)). It is therefore a price-comparison snapshot, not a bookable live rate. Pricing is public and self-serve: $350/month for 10,000 requests, $500/month for 50,000, $4,000/month for Enterprise with 10M API credits, and $0.10 per extra request; the trial gives 30 calls with no credit card ([Makcorps](https://www.makcorps.com/); [Makcorps Hotel Rate Intelligence](https://www.makcorps.com/hotel-rate-intelligence/)). A RapidAPI listing advertises $16/month for 100 requests, but that is inconsistent with the vendor's own $350 entry plan and should be verified before relying on it ([RapidAPI Makcorps](https://rapidapi.com/manthankool/api/makcorps-hotel-price-comparison/pricing)). Makcorps' public terms are generic website terms dated 2021; they restrict commercial use and public display of "the materials" and do not contain API-specific SLA, caching or redistribution terms ([Makcorps terms](https://www.makcorps.com/terms/)). No Sri Lanka-specific coverage list is published.

**Xotelo.** Xotelo is a free, lightweight API that returns OTA rates "based with TripAdvisor website" ([Xotelo](https://xotelo.com/)). `/rates` and `/list` work without a key; `/search` is restricted to RapidAPI ([live `/search` response](https://data.xotelo.com/api/search?query=Colombo&location_type=accommodation)). The documented rate response is `code`, `name`, `rate` and optionally `tax` per OTA, with a UNIX timestamp; there is no room type, board, cancellation, availability count or booking URL ([Xotelo](https://xotelo.com/)). RapidAPI pricing on 2026-10-27 was free for 1,000 requests/month, $29.99/month for 30,000, and $99.99/month for 250,000, with $0.002 overage on the Pro plan ([Xotelo RapidAPI pricing](https://rapidapi.com/anastue-pGK7lGUO-Wo/api/xotelo-hotel-prices/pricing)). A live Sri Lanka check on 2026-10-27 returned 1,936 properties for Kandy (`g304138`) and 804 for Colombo (`g293962`); for Grand Serendib Hotel, Kandy, 10–12 Nov 2026, 1 room/2 adults, it returned Booking.com 204, Agoda 138, Trip.com 93 and Vio.com 137 USD per night, with `tax: null` for all four ([live Kandy rate query](https://data.xotelo.com/api/rates?hotel_key=g304138-d25367549&chk_in=2026-11-10&chk_out=2026-11-12&rooms=1&adults=2&currency=USD)). That proves the underlying OTAs price Sri Lankan hotels, but the spread (93 vs 204 for the same dates) also shows it is not a normalized room/board comparison. The service is individual-run, with no terms page or SLA found on the site; treat it as a prototype/data-exploration tool, not a production dependency.

**Travelpayouts / Hotellook.** The Hotellook affiliate program was closed on 20 October 2025; the brand is being discontinued. Affiliate links now redirect to Booking.com, widgets and landing pages stopped working, and the Hotellook API was fully disabled — requests return an error. Travelpayouts says no other hotel brand currently offers an API to its partners ([Travelpayouts closure FAQ](https://support.travelpayouts.com/hc/en-us/articles/29534131568530-FAQ-on-the-closure-of-Hotellook)). There is no 2026 Hotellook option to compare; any article still listing it is stale.

**RateHawk (Emerging Travel Group).** RateHawk's API exposes 3.3M+ accommodations in 220 countries from 330+ suppliers, with a Content API, real-time availability, package rates, credit-card payments and a sandbox ([RateHawk API](https://www.ratehawk.com/lp/en-us/API/)). It is a net-rate B2B model: the partner pays the net rate, sets its own markup, and keeps the difference; there is no commission option ([RateHawk finance models](https://blog.ratehawk.com/types-of-ratehawk-finance-models/)). Search rates have a recommended 30-minute lifetime and a 38-minute `match_hash` lifetime, so a production integration must re-price before booking ([RateHawk hotelpage docs](https://docs.emergingtravel.com/docs/affiliate-api/hotel-search/retrieve-hotelpage/); [RateHawk search docs](https://docs.emergingtravel.com/docs/b2b-api/hotel-search/search-by-hotel-ids/)). API access itself is free; the sandbox is free, but production requires partner registration, a credit assessment and certification, typically 14–30 days for certification and 6–10 weeks to launch ([RateHawk sandbox announcement](https://blog.ratehawk.com/introducing-the-ratehawk-api-sandbox/); [ZentrumHub integration guide, Apr 2026](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/ratehawk-hotel-api-guide/)). No per-call fee or minimum volume is published. Sri Lanka is inside the 220-country footprint, but no country-level property count is published.

**Expedia Rapid API.** Rapid is Expedia Group's B2B API family; the Lodging Shopping API returns live rates and availability for 700,000+ accommodations, up to 250 properties per request, including room types, refundability, cancellation penalties and a full price breakdown ([Expedia Shopping API](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/shopping/about-shopping-api)). Access is not self-serve: you must become an Expedia partner, be approved to integrate, get a development key, pass a site review, and then have your key enabled for production ([Expedia getting started](https://developers.expediagroup.com/docs/products/rapid/setup/getting-started)). The commercial model is commissionable sell rates, including B2B distribution rates that are typically equivalent to standard rates but carry higher margins; Expedia describes its B2B partners as operating on a sell-rate model, not a net-rate model ([Expedia B2B distribution rates](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/b2b-distribution-rates-independent-regional-chain-hotels)). The Shopping response also exposes `minimum_selling_price`, `gross_profit` and `marketing_fee`, which indicate a price floor and an estimated margin rather than a confidential net rate ([Expedia Shopping API](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/shopping/about-shopping-api)). A secondary integration guide estimates 12–20 weeks to production ([ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/ratehawk-hotel-api-guide/)). No per-call fee or public minimum is published, but the application is judged on business case and projected booking volume; data-only use cases do not fit ([StayAPI Expedia analysis, Aug 2026](https://stayapi.com/blog/expedia-rapid-api)).

**Booking.com Demand API.** The Demand API is Booking.com's partner API for accommodations, cars and flights. Access requires a **Managed Affiliate Partner** account, a signed contract, Partner Centre access, an API key and an `X-Affiliate-Id`; there is no self-serve key ([Booking prerequisites](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/prerequisites); [authentication](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/development-guide/authentication)). It returns Booking.com inventory only — search, availability, room/rate detail, board, cancellation and charges — and the `book` price is the price the traveller pays; the commissionable price must not be displayed publicly ([Booking display prices](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/accommodations/display-prices)). Two restrictions are decisive for a price-comparison product: partners must not cache availability or prices ("never store dynamic data"), and must not change the price ([Booking production checklist](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/development-guide/production-readiness); [Booking usage rules](https://legacy.developers.booking.com/api/commercial/index.html?page_url=permitted-use)). Booking's partner agreement also prohibits sublicensing its materials to allow "price/availability comparison or (meta)search" ([Booking partner agreement PDF](https://image.email.partnerships.booking.com/lib/fe3011737364047d711079/m/1/55ae2c99-3af3-40b2-b57b-660c6baa6643.pdf)). The commission model is a 25–40% split of Booking's agency commission depending on monthly materialised transactions ([same partner agreement](https://image.email.partnerships.booking.com/lib/fe3011737364047d711079/m/1/55ae2c99-3af3-40b2-b57b-660c6baa6643.pdf)). No per-call fee or published minimum.

**Agoda.** Agoda has two relevant routes. The public affiliate program pays 4% (1–50 monthly departures), 4.5% (51–300) or 5% (301–1,000+), with some marketing material citing up to 7%; a website is required and applications are manually reviewed ([Agoda affiliate](https://partners.agoda.com/); [Agoda affiliate FAQ](https://partners.agoda.com/en-us/faq.html); [Agoda affiliate agreement](https://partners.agoda.com/Content/pdf/Agoda_OnlineAffiliateAgreement.pdf)). On the API side, Agoda's "Online Affiliates/MSE" model exposes a single Search API for fast availability/rates with a landing URL; the full Demand API adds Precheck and Book ([Agoda developer getting started](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs/getting-started)). Search accepts up to 100 hotel IDs per request and returns rooms, offers, board, cancellation, taxes and a `landingUrl` when `META_SEARCH` is requested ([Agoda Search API](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs/json-search-api)). Agoda's own support centre describes Lite API integration as "partner self-service", while the full API is account-managed ([Agoda Partner Center](https://agodapartners.zohodesk.com/portal/en/kb/articles/affiliate-long-tail-search-api)). Rates are retail/commission-based, so the platform cannot reprice them. The live Xotelo test returned an Agoda rate for a Kandy hotel, but no API coverage count is published.

**Trip.com.** Trip.com publishes an affiliate API at `apidoc.trip.com`; requests are HTTP POST to `openservice.ctrip.com` with `AllianceID`, `SID`, `token`/`userKey`, `icode` and a UUID, all based on Trip-issued partner credentials — there is no self-serve key generation shown ([Trip.com API docs](https://apidoc.trip.com/apidoc)). The response includes hotel availability, room name, bed type, `RoomRates` with tax-inclusive `TotalPrice`, `Inclusions` (e.g. free breakfast), `RulesandRestrictions` (refundable, cancellation policy) and a mandatory `BookURL` that sends the user to Trip.com ([Trip.com API docs](https://apidoc.trip.com/apidoc)). The public affiliate program pays 5% for 0–199 monthly hotel bookings, 6% for 200–999 and 7% for 1,000+, and its open signup is for links/widgets and commission tracking ([Trip.com partners](https://www.trip.com/partners/); [Trip.com affiliate FAQ](https://www.trip.com/ask/questions/trip.com-affliate-program.html)). So Trip.com has a genuine rate endpoint, but it is an affiliate/redirect model, not a white-label booking API; a secondary analysis describes the open affiliate program as links/widgets with no data endpoints and the distribution/supplier APIs as contract-gated ([StayAPI Trip.com analysis, Aug 2026](https://stayapi.com/blog/trip-com-api-documentation)). The live Xotelo test returned a Trip.com rate for the Kandy hotel, which is evidence of Sri Lanka inventory but not of API coverage.

**Hotelbeds (HBX Group).** Hotelbeds' API suite covers 250,000 hotels, with separate activities and transfers APIs ([Hotelbeds Hotels API suite](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/)); registration gives a free evaluation key with a 50-request/day quota, and production requires a commercial agreement and certification ([Hotelbeds getting started](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/getting-started/); [certification](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/certification-process/)). Rates are either net or commissionable; in the net model you can add your own markup unless `hotelMandatory` is true, in which case you must respect `sellingRate` ([Hotelbeds pricing models](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/pricing-models/)). Availability, CheckRate, booking, cancellation and related operations now require mutual TLS (mTLS) ([Hotelbeds mutual authentication](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/mutual-authentication/)). That is compatible with the platform's Cloudflare Workers runtime: Workers can present a client certificate through an `mtls_certificates` binding, whose `fetch()` uses the certificate ([Cloudflare Workers mTLS](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/bindings/mtls/)). The caveat is that Workers mTLS cannot be used against a destination that is itself a Cloudflare-proxied zone; verify `api-mtls.hotelbeds.com` is not proxied before relying on direct calls. The Cache API provides an hourly CSV/ZIP snapshot of prices and availability for high-volume or price-comparison use cases ([Hotelbeds Cache API](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/cache-api/)). The platform already has a fixture-tested Hotelbeds adapter (internal `docs/status.md`); the remaining work is credentials, sandbox testing, an mTLS binding and certificate lifecycle, and certification. No public Sri Lanka property count.

**Amadeus.** This is the biggest change since the platform was designed: Amadeus decommissioned its Self-Service developer portal on **17 July 2026**. New registrations were paused in March 2026, all Self-Service API keys were disabled on 17 July, and the portal now serves only the Enterprise API Portal; Enterprise APIs remain available under contract ([PhocusWire, 9 Feb 2026](https://www.phocuswire.com/amadeus-shut-down-self-service-apis-portal-developers); corroborated by [Airlabs](https://airlabs.co/amadeus-self-service-api-shutdown) and [StayAPI](https://stayapi.com/blog/amadeus-api-alternatives)). Before closure, Self-Service Hotel Search was priced at about €0.015 per request after a 3,000-request monthly free quota, with hotel booking free ([Zoftify](https://zoftify.com/blog/travel-apis); [AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/hotel-api/)). The platform's Amadeus adapter was written against the Self-Service OpenAPI specs and has never run live; with Self-Service gone, it cannot be used with new self-service credentials. Amadeus still markets 150,000 hotels on its developer home page ([Amadeus for Developers](https://developers.amadeus.com/)), but that inventory is now Enterprise-only. The only Amadeus path now is an Enterprise contract, which a secondary integration guide estimates at 10–18 weeks ([ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/ratehawk-hotel-api-guide/)).

### Inferences
- The market splits cleanly: three cross-OTA data products (SerpApi, Makcorps, Xotelo) and seven single-supplier booking/supply APIs. Only the first group can replace SerpApi's *comparison* function; the second group can replace its *bookability*, not its cross-OTA prices.
- Makcorps and Xotelo are price snapshots, not bookable rates. Neither documents room type, board, cancellation, availability count or a rate key, so neither can safely drive a checkout or a final quoted price without a second source.
- Amadeus is no longer a self-serve option. The existing adapter is effectively dead for new credentials; the platform should either negotiate Enterprise or remove Amadeus from the near-term plan.
- Hotelbeds is the strongest production supply asset the platform already has; RateHawk is the most accessible net-rate alternative if a partnership is approved.
- Sri Lanka coverage is proven for the OTA-affiliated products (Xotelo's live Kandy test returned Booking, Agoda, Trip.com and Vio rates) but not documented at country level for any API. Absence of a country count is not evidence of poor coverage.

### Gaps
- No provider publishes Sri Lanka-specific API coverage counts, so relative depth in Sri Lanka cannot be verified without test credentials.
- Vendor inventory counts conflict: RateHawk's own page says 3.3M accommodations/330+ suppliers, while a 2026 integration guide says 2.5M/200+ ([ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/ratehawk-hotel-api-guide/)); Hotelbeds' API-suite page says 250,000 hotels, while its developer home page says 300,000 ([Hotelbeds developer home](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/)). The note uses the vendor's own, more specific API-suite figures.
- Makcorps and Xotelo publish no API-specific terms, SLA, rate limits or redistribution licence; their commercial-use status is unresolved.
- RateHawk, Expedia, Booking, Agoda, Trip.com, Hotelbeds and Amadeus do not publish per-call fees or minimum volumes; contract pricing is opaque.
- Amadeus Enterprise pricing and onboarding requirements are not public.
- Hotelbeds' mTLS is supportable from Cloudflare Workers via an `mtls_certificates` binding, but Workers mTLS fails against a destination that is itself a Cloudflare-proxied zone; whether `api-mtls.hotelbeds.com` is proxied is unverified ([Cloudflare Workers mTLS](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/bindings/mtls/)). If it is, the adapter will need a proxy or a non-Workers booking service.
- The Makcorps RapidAPI $16/100-request listing conflicts with Makcorps' own $350/10k pricing and may be a different/legacy product.

## 2. Which options are realistically self-serve, and which require a partnership or volume commitment?

### Takeaway
Only **SerpApi, Makcorps and Xotelo** are available to a small platform without a commercial partnership. Agoda and Trip.com have open affiliate applications, but those are link/widget programs, not cross-OTA price APIs; their real rate APIs are partner-gated. Booking.com, Expedia, RateHawk, Hotelbeds and Amadeus Enterprise are sales-gated. Hotelbeds is the only one the platform has already integrated.

### Cited Findings
- **SerpApi**: self-serve API key, month-to-month, no contract; free tier of 250 searches ([SerpApi pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing)). Already live on the platform (internal `docs/hotel-search-implementation.md`).
- **Makcorps**: self-serve signup, 30 free calls, Stripe billing, no credit card for trial; subscription can be cancelled from the dashboard ([Makcorps](https://www.makcorps.com/)). The response is price/tax only, with no room, board, cancellation or availability data ([Makcorps hotel-ID endpoint](https://www.makcorps.com/documentation/hotel-api-search-by-hotel-id/)).
- **Xotelo**: no key needed for `/rates` and `/list`; RapidAPI key for `/search`; free and low-cost paid plans ([Xotelo](https://xotelo.com/); [RapidAPI](https://rapidapi.com/anastue-pGK7lGUO-Wo/api/xotelo-hotel-prices/pricing)). No contract, SLA or support commitment was found ([Xotelo](https://xotelo.com/)).
- **Agoda**: affiliate signup requires a website, manual review and domain verification; commission is 4–7%; the Lite API is described as partner self-service, the full Demand API is account-managed ([Agoda affiliate FAQ](https://partners.agoda.com/en-us/faq.html); [Agoda Partner Center](https://agodapartners.zohodesk.com/portal/en/kb/articles/affiliate-long-tail-search-api); [Agoda developer getting started](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs/getting-started)). Applications are manually reviewed.
- **Trip.com**: affiliate signup is open, but the rate API needs Trip-issued `AllianceID`, `SID`, `token`/`userKey` and `icode`; there is no self-serve credential generation ([Trip.com API docs](https://apidoc.trip.com/apidoc); [Trip.com partners](https://www.trip.com/partners/)).
- **Booking.com**: only Managed Affiliate Partners get Demand API credentials; the prerequisite checklist starts with a signed contract and an account manager ([Booking prerequisites](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/prerequisites)).
- **Expedia Rapid**: must become an Expedia partner, be approved, get a development key, pass a site review and have the key enabled for production ([Expedia getting started](https://developers.expediagroup.com/docs/products/rapid/setup/getting-started)). A secondary analysis says data-only use cases do not fit the application ([StayAPI](https://stayapi.com/blog/expedia-rapid-api)).
- **RateHawk**: register, get a free API key, complete sandbox integration and certification; a personal manager sends a pre-integration questionnaire, and certification typically takes 14–30 days ([RateHawk sandbox announcement](https://blog.ratehawk.com/introducing-the-ratehawk-api-sandbox/)). A credit assessment sets the credit line ([ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/ratehawk-hotel-api-guide/)).
- **Hotelbeds**: free evaluation key (50 requests/day) is self-serve, but live credentials need a commercial agreement and certification ([Hotelbeds getting started](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/getting-started/); [certification](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/certification-process/)). Already integrated, so the marginal work is an mTLS binding and certificate lifecycle, certification and commercial terms.
- **Amadeus**: no self-serve route after 17 July 2026; Enterprise only ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/amadeus-shut-down-self-service-apis-portal-developers)).

### Inferences
- A small platform can start today with SerpApi + Makcorps + Xotelo without asking anyone for permission. Of those, only SerpApi is suitable for customer-facing production; Makcorps/Xotelo are internal comparison or fallback tools.
- Agoda and Trip.com are the most accessible *affiliate* routes, but they solve "send the user to an OTA and earn commission", not "show cross-OTA prices in our own checkout". Trip.com's rate API, Booking's Demand API and Expedia Rapid are partner-gated; none of the pages reviewed shows a self-serve credential path. They are not substitutes for SerpApi.
- RateHawk is the most realistic net-rate supply partnership for a small platform: no IATA requirement, free sandbox, certification in weeks, net rates with markup ([ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/ratehawk-hotel-api-guide/)). Expedia and Booking are heavier, business-case-driven approvals.
- Hotelbeds is already the platform's best-positioned supply partner; finishing the mTLS binding and certification is likely higher ROI than adding another supply API.
- Do not plan around Amadeus Self-Service. If Amadeus is required, start an Enterprise conversation now; otherwise remove it from the provider registry.

### Gaps
- No provider publishes approval criteria, rejection reasons or minimum booking volumes. "Sales-gated" is confirmed, but the bar is opaque.
- Agoda/Trip.com full API terms (as opposed to affiliate terms) are not public; it is unclear whether a small platform can get the full booking API without a meaningful volume commitment.
- RateHawk's credit-line minimum and Hotelbeds' commercial thresholds are contract-specific and not published.
- Booking/Expedia may require a functioning booking flow and site review; a price-only integration would likely be rejected, but this is inferred from their launch requirements and secondary reporting, not from a published rejection rule.

## 3. Are prices net or retail? Can they be displayed or repriced? What caching/display restrictions apply?

### Takeaway
Only **Hotelbeds** and **RateHawk** give net rates that a platform can mark up freely (subject to `hotelMandatory` and contract terms). **Expedia** is a sell-rate model with a `minimum_selling_price` floor, so it may allow limited repricing above that floor. **Booking.com, Agoda and Trip.com** are retail/commission models that prohibit changing the price. Makcorps and Xotelo are retail scraped/aggregated prices with no clear commercial redistribution licence. Booking.com goes further: it prohibits caching availability/prices and restricts use of its content for price comparison or metasearch.

### Cited Findings

| Provider | Rate basis | Markup / repricing | Caching and display restrictions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SerpApi Google Hotels** | Retail OTA/Google prices | No — display the public price as-is | 1-hour cache; 31-day data retention; U.S. Legal Shield covers scraping/parsing, not downstream use; no explicit redistribution licence |
| **Makcorps** | Retail OTA price + tax | No — data only, no booking path | No API-specific terms found; generic website terms restrict commercial use/public display of "the materials"; no caching/SLA guidance |
| **Xotelo** | Retail OTA rate per room/night; `tax` often null | No — data only | No terms page/SLA found; TripAdvisor-derived; no caching or redistribution licence |
| **RateHawk** | Net | Yes — add markup; never show net to the traveller | 30-minute recommended rate lifetime; 38-minute `match_hash`; net pricing confidential under B2B contract |
| **Hotelbeds** | Net or commissionable | Yes in net model, unless `hotelMandatory=true`; then use `sellingRate` | Net prices are B2B/confidential; Cache API snapshot is hourly; availability and booking calls require mTLS |
| **Expedia Rapid** | Sell rates (commissionable) with a `minimum_selling_price` floor | Limited — sell at or above the floor; `gross_profit` is the estimated margin; contract-dependent | Expedia launch/display requirements; partner terms; not a confidential net-rate feed |
| **Booking.com Demand** | Retail `book` price; commissionable price is internal | No — "No Changing Prices" | Must not cache availability/prices; must not use Booking content for price comparison/metasearch; commissionable price must not be public |
| **Agoda** | Retail/commissionable | No — price is the sell rate; earn commission | MSE requires `landingUrl`; display/booking rules in affiliate agreement |
| **Trip.com** | Retail, tax-inclusive | No — price is the sell rate; earn commission | `BookURL` is mandatory; redirect to Trip.com |
| **Amadeus** | GDS/contract (net or commissionable depending on deal) | Enterprise contract-dependent | Self-Service closed; Enterprise terms govern |

- SerpApi caches identical queries for one hour and does not count cached searches; search data is retained for 31 days, with ZeroTrace available only on Enterprise/Cloud-volume plans (the API docs say "Enterprise only"; the pricing page shows 1M+ searches/month) ([SerpApi Google Hotels API](https://serpapi.com/google-hotels-api); [SerpApi legal](https://serpapi.com/legal); [SerpApi pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing)). The Legal Shield is explicitly limited to "lawful collection of public search data", not to how the customer uses it ([SerpApi legal](https://serpapi.com/legal)). There is no stated right to redistribute the data as a standalone feed.
- Booking.com's production checklist says: "Never store dynamic data. Do not store prices or availability, as these change frequently" ([Booking production checklist](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/development-guide/production-readiness)). Its usage rules say partners may not cache availability or prices and may not change the price; they also allow an affiliate to compare prices only if it uses its own property content, not Booking's descriptions, photos, facilities or policies ([Booking usage rules](https://legacy.developers.booking.com/api/commercial/index.html?page_url=permitted-use)). The demand display guide says the commissionable price "should not be displayed publicly" ([Booking display prices](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/accommodations/display-prices)). The partner agreement prohibits sublicensing Booking materials to allow "price/availability comparison or (meta)search" ([Booking partner agreement PDF](https://image.email.partnerships.booking.com/lib/fe3011737364047d711079/m/1/55ae2c99-3af3-40b2-b57b-660c6baa6643.pdf)).
- RateHawk's net model lets the partner set a markup and keep the difference; the API returns net and gross amounts, and the docs warn that the rate lifetime is about 30 minutes, so cached prices cannot be shown as final ([RateHawk finance models](https://blog.ratehawk.com/types-of-ratehawk-finance-models/); [RateHawk hotelpage docs](https://docs.emergingtravel.com/docs/affiliate-api/hotel-search/retrieve-hotelpage/)).
- Hotelbeds' pricing model distinguishes net and commissionable. In the net model, if `hotelMandatory` is true the partner must respect the `sellingRate`; otherwise it may add its own markup ([Hotelbeds pricing models](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/pricing-models/)). Its Cache API is an hourly snapshot for high-volume/price-comparison use cases ([Hotelbeds Cache API](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/cache-api/)). HBX Group's Bedsonline legal statement says its prices are confidential and may not be revealed, which is consistent with treating Hotelbeds net rates as confidential ([Bedsonline legal statement](https://discover.bedsonline.com/it-it/legal-statement)).
- Expedia's Rapid Shopping API returns sell rates, refundability, cancellation penalties, `minimum_selling_price`, `gross_profit` and `marketing_fee`; Expedia describes B2B distribution partners as operating on a sell-rate model. The presence of a minimum selling price and gross profit means limited repricing above a floor may be possible, but this is contract-governed, not an open net-rate feed ([Expedia Shopping API](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/shopping/about-shopping-api); [Expedia B2B distribution rates](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/b2b-distribution-rates-independent-regional-chain-hotels)).
- Agoda's Search API returns `landingUrl` when `META_SEARCH` is requested, and the affiliate agreement pays commission on completed stays; the rate is the sell rate, not a net rate ([Agoda Search API](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs/json-search-api); [Agoda affiliate agreement](https://partners.agoda.com/Content/pdf/Agoda_OnlineAffiliateAgreement.pdf)).
- Trip.com's response includes a mandatory `BookURL` that determines the landing page and carries the affiliate tracking; the price is the tax-inclusive retail price ([Trip.com API docs](https://apidoc.trip.com/apidoc)).
- Makcorps' terms are generic website terms, last revised January 2021, granting only a temporary personal, non-commercial viewing licence and prohibiting commercial use or public display of "the materials" ([Makcorps terms](https://www.makcorps.com/terms/)). Whether that applies to API data is ambiguous, but there is no affirmative commercial-display licence. Xotelo has no terms page at all ([Xotelo](https://xotelo.com/)).

### Inferences
- A platform cannot lawfully build a "we are cheaper than Booking.com" comparison using Booking's own API: Booking prohibits caching and price changes, and its usage rules let an affiliate compare prices only if it uses its own property content, not Booking's descriptions, photos, facilities or policies.
- The same logic makes Agoda and Trip.com poor inputs for a repricing engine, and Expedia only a limited one: Expedia is a sell-rate channel with a price floor, not a wholesale feed.
- Only Hotelbeds and RateHawk support the platform's existing pricing-engine model (cost → margin → selling price) without a price floor. Expedia may allow a margin above `minimum_selling_price`, but that is contract-dependent; the retail APIs cannot be repriced.
- Makcorps and Xotelo are not licensed price feeds. Even if the data is accurate, displaying it as the platform's own price or caching it for reuse carries contract and data-quality risk. They are suitable for internal comparison, QA and fallback only.
- SerpApi's Legal Shield reduces scraping risk but does not grant redistribution rights. A legal review of SerpApi's terms and Google's terms is still needed before displaying or storing the data at scale.
- For a customer-facing "best price" feature, the defensible architecture is: SerpApi/Google for discovery and cross-OTA comparison, plus Hotelbeds/RateHawk net rates for the actual bookable price. Do not present Makcorps/Xotelo prices as final.

### Gaps
- Makcorps and Xotelo do not publish API terms, SLA, rate limits or a commercial redistribution licence; their legal status is unresolved.
- Agoda and Trip.com do not publish cache TTLs or detailed display rules in the pages reviewed; Expedia publishes price-floor fields but not contract-level repricing rules. Their contracts may impose stricter terms than the public docs.
- SerpApi's terms do not explicitly say whether cached results may be retained beyond the 1-hour cache/31-day retention for analytics; this needs legal review.
- Tax inclusiveness is inconsistent: Xotelo returned `tax: null` for all four OTAs in the Sri Lanka test, while Trip.com documents tax-inclusive totals and Booking documents separate taxes/charges.
- Hotelbeds' `hotelMandatory` frequency by market is not public, so it is unclear how often a markup is actually permitted.

### Takeaway
At every volume tested, **Makcorps costs more in cash to cover that volume and returns less useful data**. SerpApi is $25 / $150 / $725 per month at 1k / 10k / 100k; Makcorps is $350 / $350 / $4,000–$5,500. Xotelo is far cheaper per call but is not a production-grade, licensed service. The supply APIs (Hotelbeds, RateHawk, Expedia, Booking, Agoda, Trip.com) have no per-search fee but require bookings and weeks of integration, so their cost is transactional, not comparable per search.

At 100k, the cheapest single plan that covers the volume is Enterprise at $4,000, or $5,500 on Advance plus 50k overage. The $4,000 is a plan minimum that includes 10M credits, so the apparent $0.04/search at 100k is not the credit price — at full utilisation the credits are $0.0004 each. This comparison therefore measures **cash cost to cover the volume**, not Makcorps' marginal unit price. That is **14× SerpApi at 1k, 2.3× at 10k and 5.5–7.6× at 100k** on that basis.

One rate call per search gives $0/$0.003/$0.001 per search. But a destination search needs one `/list` call plus one `/rates` call per hotel; at 20 hotels per search, 100k searches is about 2.1M calls, which is 8.4× the Ultra plan's 250k allowance. The $899.91 figure assumes nine Ultra subscriptions; RapidAPI may instead require a custom plan or overage, so treat it as a lower-bound estimate. At the published rates it is more than SerpApi at 100k, cheaper at 10k and roughly comparable at 1k. The direct `/rates` endpoint is free and unauthenticated, but it has no SLA or commercial terms ([Xotelo](https://xotelo.com/)).

Their cash cost at zero bookings is near zero, but so is their value; at 100k searches the cost depends on conversion and booking value, not on search count ([RateHawk finance models](https://blog.ratehawk.com/types-of-ratehawk-finance-models/); [Expedia B2B distribution rates](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/b2b-distribution-rates-independent-regional-chain-hotels); [Agoda affiliate agreement](https://partners.agoda.com/Content/pdf/Agoda_OnlineAffiliateAgreement.pdf); [Trip.com affiliate FAQ](https://www.trip.com/ask/questions/trip.com-affliate-program.html)).
- Engineering time is the hidden cost: SerpApi is already integrated; Makcorps/Xotelo are days; RateHawk is 6–10 weeks, Expedia 12–20 weeks and Amadeus Enterprise 10–18 weeks, per a secondary integration guide ([ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/ratehawk-hotel-api-guide/)). At 1k–10k searches, weeks of engineering dwarf the monthly API fees.

### Inferences
- **There is no cheaper production-grade cross-OTA replacement for SerpApi at these volumes.** Makcorps is more expensive at 1k, 10k and 100k on a cash-cost basis, and its response lacks the room/board/cancellation data needed to quote a stay. Its Enterprise plan only looks cheap per credit at multi-million-request utilisation; at 100k you are paying for unused capacity. Xotelo is cheaper per call but has no contract, SLA or commercial licence, and its per-hotel fan-out can erase the savings at 100k searches.
- SerpApi remains the correct default for discovery and cross-OTA comparison. Its $725/month at 100k searches is less than one week of a mid-level engineer and far below Makcorps' $4,000/month.
- If the platform needs bookable net rates, Hotelbeds (already integrated) and RateHawk (if approved) are the cost-effective supply layer: no per-search fee, and the margin is controlled by the platform. They are not substitutes for SerpApi's comparison data.
- Booking, Expedia, Agoda and Trip.com are not cost-effective for a price-only use case: they require a booking product, approval, and weeks of integration, and their terms restrict comparison or repricing (Expedia least so, but it is still single-supplier).
- Amadeus should be removed from the near-term provider plan or migrated to Enterprise; keeping a dead Self-Service adapter in the registry creates false confidence.

### Gaps
- Contract pricing for RateHawk, Expedia, Booking, Agoda, Trip.com, Hotelbeds and Amadeus Enterprise is not public; total cost depends on booking volume and negotiated margins.
- No vendor publishes a Sri Lanka-specific price or coverage tier; all cost figures are global list prices or contract models.
- Engineering cost is not quantified in dollars; the week estimates are secondary/inferred and vary with team experience.
- "Search" units differ: SerpApi and Makcorps city search are per destination query; Xotelo is per hotel; Hotelbeds availability can cover up to 2,000 hotel codes per call ([Hotelbeds availability API](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/booking-api/api-reference/)). The comparison is therefore directional, not invoice-exact.
- Makcorps does not publish whether one API credit equals one request; its docs say one hotel-ID call is counted as one API request, but the Enterprise plan is sold in "credits". The $4,000/100k figure treats the plan as a $4,000 minimum, not as 100k credits.

## 5. Which options are really affiliate link builders rather than price APIs?

### Takeaway
**Travelpayouts/Hotellook is dead.** **Trip.com's open affiliate route is a link/widget builder**, and its partner rate API returns a mandatory `BookURL` that redirects to Trip.com. **Agoda's affiliate Lite API is a rate API plus a landing URL** (metasearch/MSE model), while its full Demand API can complete a booking. **Booking.com's open affiliate route is links**, but its Demand API is a real booking API — just comparison-restricted. **Makcorps and Xotelo are pure data with no booking path.** **Expedia Rapid, RateHawk and Hotelbeds are true booking APIs**, not link builders.

### Cited Findings

| Provider | What it really is | Can you complete a booking in your app? | Link/redirect required? | Cross-OTA? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SerpApi Google Hotels** | Price-data API | No | No booking link required by the API, but no booking capability | Yes |
| **Makcorps** | Price-comparison data API | No | No booking URL documented | Yes |
| **Xotelo** | TripAdvisor-derived price-data API | No | No booking URL documented | Yes |
| **Travelpayouts/Hotellook** | Defunct affiliate/widget program | No | Links now redirect to Booking.com | Was yes |
| **RateHawk** | B2B booking API | Yes (search → prebook → book) | No; you sell under your own brand | No |
| **Expedia Rapid** | B2B booking API | Yes (shopping → booking) | No; you sell under your brand | No |
| **Booking.com Demand** | Affiliate/partner booking API | Yes (search, look and book) | Redirect model for affiliates; API can book | No |
| **Agoda** | Affiliate + B2B booking API | Full Demand API: yes; Lite/MSE: no (redirect) | `landingUrl` returned for MSE redirect | No |
| **Trip.com** | Affiliate + partner rate API | No white-label booking; `BookURL` redirect | `BookURL` mandatory | No |
| **Hotelbeds** | B2B booking API | Yes (availability → checkrate → book) | No; you sell under your brand | No |

- Hotellook was an affiliate/metasearch program, not a booking API. It closed on 20 October 2025; the API was disabled and links now redirect to Booking.com ([Travelpayouts closure FAQ](https://support.travelpayouts.com/hc/en-us/articles/29534131568530-FAQ-on-the-closure-of-Hotellook)). Any "Travelpayouts hotel API" recommendation in 2026 is obsolete.
- Trip.com's API is a rate endpoint, but the response's `BookURL` is described as necessary for redirect and carries the affiliate tracking; the partner cannot complete the booking in its own checkout ([Trip.com API docs](https://apidoc.trip.com/apidoc)). The open affiliate program is links/widgets and commission tracking, with 5–7% hotel commission ([Trip.com partners](https://www.trip.com/partners/); [Trip.com affiliate FAQ](https://www.trip.com/ask/questions/trip.com-affliate-program.html)).
- Agoda's "Online Affiliates/MSE" model uses only the Search API and returns a landing URL; the full Demand API adds Precheck and Book ([Agoda developer getting started](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs/getting-started); [Agoda Search API](https://developer.agoda.com/demand/docs/json-search-api)). The Lite API is self-service for approved partners; the full API is account-managed ([Agoda Partner Center](https://agodapartners.zohodesk.com/portal/en/kb/articles/affiliate-long-tail-search-api)).
- Booking.com's affiliate program is link/banner based and pays commission through networks; the Demand API is the booking API for Managed Affiliate Partners ([Booking affiliate program](https://www.booking.com/affiliate-program/v2/index.html); [Booking prerequisites](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/prerequisites)). The Demand API can search, look and book, but the partner agreement prohibits sublicensing Booking content for price comparison/metasearch, and the usage rules require an affiliate to use its own property content for any comparison ([Booking partner agreement PDF](https://image.email.partnerships.booking.com/lib/fe3011737364047d711079/m/1/55ae2c99-3af3-40b2-b57b-660c6baa6643.pdf); [Booking usage rules](https://legacy.developers.booking.com/api/commercial/index.html?page_url=permitted-use)).
- Expedia Rapid, RateHawk and Hotelbeds are end-to-end booking APIs: search/availability, rate confirmation, booking, cancellation. Expedia requires a site review and production key ([Expedia getting started](https://developers.expediagroup.com/docs/products/rapid/setup/getting-started)); RateHawk requires certification ([RateHawk sandbox](https://blog.ratehawk.com/introducing-the-ratehawk-api-sandbox/)); Hotelbeds requires certification and mTLS ([Hotelbeds certification](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/certification-process/); [Hotelbeds mTLS](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/documentation/hotels/knowledge-base/mutual-authentication/)).
- Makcorps and Xotelo document no booking URL, rate key or booking endpoint; they are data-only ([Makcorps hotel-ID endpoint](https://www.makcorps.com/documentation/hotel-api-search-by-hotel-id/); [Xotelo](https://xotelo.com/)).

### Inferences
- If the product requirement is "show cross-OTA prices and let the user book", no single provider solves it. The practical stack is SerpApi (discovery/comparison) + Hotelbeds/RateHawk (bookable net rates), with the platform's pricing engine choosing the sell price. Affiliate APIs (Trip.com, Agoda Lite) can monetise outbound clicks but cannot replace the comparison layer.
- Trip.com and Agoda are best classified as **affiliate rate/link APIs**, not price-data APIs. They return prices only to support a redirect and commission.
- Booking.com and Expedia are **booking APIs**, not price-comparison APIs. Their approval processes and display rules assume a transactional travel site, not a data dashboard.
- Makcorps and Xotelo are **pure price-data APIs**; they cannot be the fulfillment layer. Using them for customer-facing prices without a booking path creates a dead end and a price-accuracy risk.

### Gaps
- Trip.com's and Agoda's full API commercial terms (volume expectations, white-label rights, caching) are not public.
- It is unclear whether Agoda's MSE model permits all price-comparison layouts or only its approved widgets/landing pages.
- Booking.com's affiliate-vs-Demand-API eligibility for a small platform is not public; the prerequisite is a managed partner contract, but the approval bar is undocumented.
- No provider publishes a definitive statement on whether its prices may be stored for analytics (as opposed to displayed); this needs legal review.

### Bottom line
Keep **SerpApi** for cross-OTA discovery and comparison: at 1k / 10k / 100k searches it is $25 / $150 / $725 per month, and Makcorps costs more at those tiers while returning less (no room, board, cancellation or availability data); Makcorps' 10M-credit Enterprise plan only becomes cheaper per search at multi-million-request volumes. Use **Hotelbeds** (already integrated; add an mTLS binding) and, if approved, **RateHawk** for bookable net rates and margin control. Treat **Makcorps and Xotelo** as internal QA/prototype sources, not customer-facing production feeds. Do not plan on **Travelpayouts/Hotellook** (closed) or **Amadeus Self-Service** (decommissioned 17 July 2026); **Booking.com, Expedia, Agoda and Trip.com** are single-supplier or affiliate channels, not cross-OTA price substitutes.
